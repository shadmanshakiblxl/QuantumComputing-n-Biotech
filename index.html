01 / Architecture

Classical computers vs quantum computers

Classical computer

Uses bits

Each bit has a definite value: 0 or 1. Your laptop, smartphone, and servers rely on classical computing architecture.

Quantum computer

Uses qubits

A qubit can be in a superposition of 0 and 1 until measured, with measurement probabilities determined by its quantum state.

Important: a qubit is not simply a bit that stores both answers in the ordinary sense. Quantum states follow different mathematical rules, and measurement produces a classical result.

02 / Principles

The three fundamental concepts

A. Superposition

A classical bit is either 0 or 1. A qubit can exist in a combination of both possible states simultaneously.

Classical bit 0 or 1
Qubit state a|0> + b|1>
Here, a and b are probability amplitudes satisfying |a|^2 + |b|^2 = 1. When measured, the qubit returns 0 with probability |a|^2, or 1 with probability |b|^2. Quantum algorithms manipulate these superpositions to influence output probabilities.

B. Entanglement

Entanglement is a special quantum relationship between two or more particles or qubits. When qubits are entangled, their joint quantum state cannot be fully described as independent states of each qubit.

State representation: (|00> + |11>) / sqrt(2)

When measured in the same basis, their results match: either 00 or 11. This does not allow faster-than-light communication, but gives quantum computers correlations classical systems cannot reproduce.

C. Quantum interference

Quantum states have probability amplitudes that can reinforce or cancel one another.

Constructive interference Increases the probability of certain desirable outcomes during computation.
Destructive interference Decreases the probability of incorrect or unwanted outcomes.

04 / Operations

What are quantum gates?

Quantum gates are operations that change quantum states, playing a role similar to logic gates in classical computing.

Gate Basic function
X gate Flips |0> to |1>, and vice versa.
H gate (Hadamard) Creates an equal superposition from |0>.
Z gate Changes the phase of the |1> component.
CNOT gate Flips a target qubit depending on a control qubit.
05 / Impact

Why are quantum computers important?

Drug discovery

Simulate complex molecular and chemical systems, potentially accelerating discoveries in pharmacology and material science.

Cryptography

Shor algorithm can efficiently factor large integers on fault-tolerant hardware, threatening current RSA encryption standards.

Materials science

Investigate superconductors, high-capacity batteries, advanced catalysts, and novel complex materials.

Optimization

Offer theoretical advantages for complex search and logistics optimization problems across multiple industries.

07 / Convergence

How quantum computing can merge with biotech

Biological systems operate at the molecular and atomic scale, where quantum mechanical effects govern interactions such as electron tunneling, molecular bonding, and energy transfer. Merging quantum computing with biotechnology bridges theoretical physics and living systems.

Molecular modeling

Classical computers struggle to simulate complex molecular structures because the quantum states of electrons grow exponentially with system size. Quantum algorithms natively compute molecular orbitals and energy states with absolute precision.

Protein folding precision

Proteins fold into intricate three-dimensional shapes driven by quantum forces. Quantum systems can evaluate countless folding conformations simultaneously through superposition, uncovering precise structures behind diseases.

Enzyme optimization

Designing synthetic enzymes for carbon capture or targeted therapeutics requires analyzing transition states that classical systems approximate poorly. Quantum computation calculates catalytic mechanisms at atomic fidelity.

08 / Frontiers

How far can biotech go with quantum computing

The integration of quantum mechanics into biological research opens horizons previously constrained by computational limits, transitioning biology from empirical trial-and-error to exact quantum prediction.

Biological complexity at scale

Future biotechnology can model entire cellular networks, accounting for multi-protein interactions and genomic feedback loops concurrently rather than isolated components.

Accelerated drug development cycles

By replacing years of laboratory screening with exact quantum simulations, pharmaceutical design timelines can shrink from decades to days, predicting toxicity and efficacy before synthesis.

True personalized medicine

Quantum biotech will enable real-time simulation of individual patient genetic profiles against bespoke molecular therapies, completely eliminating adverse drug reactions.

09 / Achievement

How far can a quantum-bio system achieve

When quantum systems synchronize with biological principles, humanity unlocks capabilities that transcend traditional biological limitations.

Photosynthetic quantum coherence

Natural photosynthesis exploits quantum coherence to transfer energy with near-100 percent efficiency. Replicating this in synthetic systems can yield revolutionary solar energy conversion technologies.

Synthetic biology breakthroughs

Engineered organisms programmed at the quantum level to synthesize complex biopolymers, clean toxic industrial waste, or produce carbon-neutral fuels with unprecedented efficiency.

Absolute binding affinity prediction

Achieving absolute accuracy in predicting ligand-receptor binding affinity, solving age-old biochemical bottlenecks in neurodegenerative disease treatments and oncology.
